Synthetic fMRI & Brain Geometry

Generating biologically realistic brain activity from geometry.

I'm working on constructing a bijective map between fMRI signals and their spectral representations in the geometric eigenmode basis of the brain. Then, I plan to train two types of generative models on thousands of scans from publicly available datasets: a conditional variational autoencoder (cVAE) and a diffusion model (the same family of methods behind modern AI image generators).

Ideally, it would help to learn how brain geometry relates to functional activity and generate biologically realistic patterns of brain activity.

  • Geometric eigenmodes - Break brain activity into natural patterns set by the brain's own shape, so neighbouring regions stay connected.
  • cVAE - A generative model that learns to compress brain activity and create new examples for a given brain shape.
  • Diffusion models - The method behind modern image generators, adapted to build realistic brain activity step by step from random noise.
  • Brain-shape conditioning - Each synthetic scan is tailored to one specific individual's brain shape.
  • Physics-based baseline - A model of how activity spreads across the brain's surface, used as a benchmark to beat.
  • Validation - Synthetic scans are compared with real recordings to check they behave like real brains.
  • Privacy checks - Tests confirm the synthetic scans don't reproduce anyone from the training data.
