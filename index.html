Synthetic fMRI & Brain Geometry

Generating biologically realistic brain activity from geometry.

fMRI scans are expensive to collect, slow to share because they contain sensitive medical data, and often too scarce to train modern AI systems effectively. Synthetic data could help fill that gap.

The goal of this project is to generate biologically realistic synthetic brain-activity scans using brain geometry as the starting point, with a particular interest in unusual or underrepresented brain geometries.

I'm working on constructing a bijective map between fMRI signals and their spectral representations in the geometric eigenmode basis of the brain. Using this representation, I plan to train two types of generative models on thousands of scans from publicly available datasets: a conditional variational autoencoder (cVAE) and a diffusion model (the same family of methods behind modern AI image generators).

The idea is to learn how brain geometry relates to functional activity and ultimately generate biologically realistic patterns of brain activity conditioned on a given brain geometry.

  • Spectral geometry — representing brain activity through geometric eigenmodes
  • Bijective mapping — constructing an invertible transformation between fMRI signals and their spectral representations
  • Generative modelling — geometry-conditioned variational autoencoders and diffusion models
  • Synthetic data — generating realistic fMRI scans for rare and underrepresented brain geometries
